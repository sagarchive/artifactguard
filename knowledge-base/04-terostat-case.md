# Terostat adhesive case

A 2025 review reports that a structural adhesive, Terostat-9220, applied by a casework manufacturer from 2009 through 2014, passed an Oddy test conducted by museum staff because it does not react corrosively with the metal substrates. The source of TMP-ol was later identified as that adhesive. White crystalline efflorescence appeared on objects in display cases at several institutions, caused by a reaction between volatile 2,2,6,6-tetramethyl-4-piperidinol (TMP-ol) and acidic compounds from other materials and objects in the cases.

The primary study at the Smithsonian's National Museum of the American Indian identified TMP-ol as emitted by the structural adhesive Terostat MS 937. It reports that the case manufacturer used two formulations, Terostat MS 937 and Terostat 9220, and that only Terostat 937 has been found in the NMAI cases. The two sources therefore name the formulation differently, and the cited passages do not say which formulation was Oddy tested.

The narrow conclusion is not that Oddy testing is useless. A screening test on metal coupons did not identify an emitted compound or its reaction with acids, so a single PASS did not establish compatibility of the whole display system.
